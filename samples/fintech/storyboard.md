# Court Mein Hisaab
Chukta bill-splitting app | role_play | Instagram Reels | Hinglish | 22 s + end card (target 22 s)

## Concept
**Court Mein Hisaab** (score 42/50). Role play in one living room. One friend plays judge with a steel tumbler as gavel, the other plays the accused flatmate who keeps saying 'kal de dunga'. Judge sighs and says the court has no time for this, then holds up the phone showing the Chukta group where everyone can see who owes what. The app sends the reminder automatically, so the judge never has to ask. Accused settles by UPI inside the app on the spot. Judge bangs the tumbler: 'Case dismissed.' End card: Chukta, free to download.
Why it won: The courtroom hook is instantly funny and relatable, and the steel tumbler gavel is a lovely Indian-household touch. It covers the group view, auto-reminders and UPI settle-up. Two people in one room is easy for AI video; only the phone screen needs care.

## Look
- **mood**: playful mock-seriousness that melts into relief: a fake courtroom with real friends laughing underneath (The concept runs on a straight-faced joke. The awkwardness of asking for money has to turn into a light release when the app settles it.)
- **tone**: flatmates teasing each other in Hinglish, a mock-solemn judge against a cheeky accused. It never sounds like a bank or a lecture. (The audience trusts friends, not fintech. The teasing lets the ad name an awkward problem without shaming anyone.)
- **setting**: a lived-in Indian flat living room with a sagging sofa, a plastic-covered side table, a drying rack in the corner, and a steel tumbler and a half-eaten snack plate on the table (It looks like the places where rent and food get split. The steel tumbler gavel is a familiar household prop and the scene needs no extra set.)
- **camera**: handheld smartphone front camera look, shot and reverse shot between judge and accused, with one short over-the-shoulder shot on the phone screen (It feels like a Reel a friend filmed. Reverse shots keep the back-and-forth quick and make the app screen readable once.)
- **lens**: smartphone front camera, 24 mm equivalent, f/1.9, at arm's length (It is the lens people already use for Reels and gives a slightly wide, intimate frame. Two people in a small room fit without looking staged.)
- **light**: late-afternoon daylight from a window on the left, with a warm tube-light bounce from the ceiling (One real window source keeps skin tones natural and the room looks lived-in. Warm indoor bounce adds the Indian flat feel and keeps the phone screen bright by contrast.)
- **palette**: muted everyday tones of faded sofa teal, wood brown, steel grey and cream walls (A plain room makes the app the brightest thing in frame, so the eye lands on the product at the moment of the group view and UPI settle-up.)
- **pacing**: cuts every 3-4 seconds: hook in the first 3s, two quick 'kal de dunga' exchanges, the phone reveal around 10s, UPI settle and 'Case dismissed' by 19s, end card for the last 3s (This fits the 22s target and the Reels scroll. Each beat gets one clear shot and the payoff lands before attention drops.)
- **brand accent** (only in shots that show the product): one bright brand-green accent on the Chukta phone screen
